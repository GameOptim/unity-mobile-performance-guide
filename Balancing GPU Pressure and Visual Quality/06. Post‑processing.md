# 4.6 Post‑processing

Post‑processing is one of the most common causes of **high GPU pressure**.

Essentially, it is full-screen rendering with **high pixel count and complex shaders**.

Below are common post effects and corresponding optimizations:

**Tonemapping**

Greatly increases Uberpost complexity.

**ACES is not recommended in LDR mode**.

In HDR mode, tonemapping can be baked into **ColorGradingLut** to avoid high-cost calculation during Uberpost.

In LDR mode, use low-cost or free alternatives to achieve similar visual results.

**Bloom**

The most widely used post effect.

It increases Uberpost shader complexity and generates multiple DrawCalls based on sample count.

Default: downsampling starts at **1/2 resolution**.

Optimization:

On mid-low-end devices, start downsampling at **1/4 resolution** to drastically reduce bandwidth cost, and **reduce downsample steps** to lower DrawCall count.

**Depth Of Field**

Expensive on mobile.

Not recommended for continuous use.

- **Bokeh**: high quality, very high cost
- **Gaussian**: lower cost, softer quality

**SMAA**

Common anti-aliasing method using 3 passes: edge detection, weight calculation, blending.

Relatively high cost — **not recommended on mid-low-end devices**.

General post‑processing principles:

- Minimize use of high-cost effects; use low-cost approximations where possible.
- Avoid **Global Volume** always enabled. Use **Local Volume** or enable only under specific logic.
- If post must be enabled, make tradeoffs: prioritize high-value effects, or save cost in other modules.
- Apply dedicated optimizations (e.g., Bloom resolution and sample count as above).

Performance and usage of post effects vary widely.

In many projects, **custom open‑source solutions** are more suitable for mobile than Unity’s built-in post-processing stack.
